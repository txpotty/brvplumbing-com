    Signs that you might need a new sewage ejector pump include unusual noises coming from the pump or pit, frequent cycling on and off, slow drainage from basement fixtures, or a sewage odor emanating from the area.     Regular maintenance for a sewage ejector pump involves periodically checking the pump basin for debris, ensuring the check valve is functioning correctly, and testing the float switch. It's also wise to avoid flushing non-biodegradable items. BRV Plumbing offers maintenance services to keep your pump running efficiently and prevent unexpected breakdowns.

    A well-maintained sewage ejector pump typically has a lifespan of 7 to 10 years, though some higher-quality units can last longer. Factors like frequency of use, quality of installation, and the type of waste being pumped can affect its longevity. Regular maintenance can help extend its operational life.

    A standard sewage ejector pump installation can typically be completed within one full day, often 4 to 8 hours, for residential properties.     A sewage ejector pump is specifically designed to handle solid waste and wastewater from plumbing fixtures below the main sewer line, like toilets and sinks, discharging it into the sewer system.
