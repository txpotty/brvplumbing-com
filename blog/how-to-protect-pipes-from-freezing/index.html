    How to Protect Your Pipes Before the Next Alabama Freeze

    By Andrew Blake · October 6, 2026 · 5 min read

    Understanding Alabama's Winter Plumbing Risks

    Here in Alabama, we're used to mild winters, but when a hard freeze hits, it can catch many homeowners off guard. Our climate means many homes aren't built with the same level of pipe insulation as those in colder northern states. Exposed pipes in crawl spaces, outside walls, or even unheated garages are particularly vulnerable. When water freezes, it expands, creating immense pressure that can burst pipes, leading to significant water damage, expensive repairs, and a major headache. Understanding these specific risks is the first step toward effective prevention.

    The Dangers of Freezing Temperatures

    While a quick dip below freezing might not cause immediate issues, sustained temperatures in the 20s or lower for several hours are when problems typically start. Many older homes in Alabama, especially those with pier-and-beam foundations, have more exposed plumbing lines than newer slab-on-grade constructions. Even with slab foundations, outdoor spigots and lines running through exterior walls can be at risk. The soil conditions in Alabama also mean that ground frost can affect shallowly buried lines if the freeze penetrates deep enough. Don't wait until the mercury drops to take action.

    Essential Steps to Winterize Your Plumbing

    Taking a few proactive steps can save you thousands of dollars in repairs and the inconvenience of a burst pipe. These measures are straightforward and can be done by most homeowners before the next cold snap arrives.

    • Disconnect Garden Hoses: This is a simple but crucial step. Hoses left connected can trap water in the spigot and supply line, which will freeze and expand, often bursting the spigot itself or the pipe inside your wall. After disconnecting, drain any remaining water from the hose and store it away.
    • Insulate Exposed Pipes: Focus on pipes in unheated areas like crawl spaces, attics, garages, and along exterior walls. Foam pipe insulation, available at any hardware store, is inexpensive and easy to install. You can also use heat tape, but follow the manufacturer's instructions carefully to avoid fire hazards. Remember that even pipes in well-insulated parts of your home can freeze if they run through an unheated space for a short distance.
    • Seal Air Leaks: Cold air infiltration through cracks in your foundation, around utility penetrations, or gaps in your exterior can expose pipes to freezing temperatures. Use caulk or expanding foam to seal these openings, especially in crawl spaces and basements. A little draft can make a big difference for an exposed water line.
    • Open Cabinet Doors: For pipes under sinks that are on exterior walls, open the cabinet doors during a freeze. This allows warmer air from your home to circulate around the pipes, helping to prevent freezing. This is a common strategy for kitchens and bathrooms in Alabama homes.
    • Let Faucets Drip: During severe freezes, a slow, steady drip from a cold water faucet can relieve pressure in your pipes, making them less likely to burst if water does freeze. This doesn't prevent freezing entirely, but it can mitigate the damage. A very thin stream, not a full flow, is all that's needed.

    When to Call a Professional

    While many winterization tasks are DIY-friendly, some situations call for the expertise of a professional plumber. If you have complex plumbing systems, suspect a leak, or are unsure about the best way to protect your specific home, don't hesitate to reach out. BRV Plumbing is ready to help homeowners across Alabama prepare for winter. Our team can assess your home's unique vulnerabilities, recommend tailored solutions, and even perform professional winterization services to give you peace of mind. If you encounter a burst pipe, shut off your main water supply immediately and call us at (256) 391-9413 for emergency repair.

    What if My Pipes Freeze?

    If you turn on a faucet and only a trickle comes out, or no water at all, you likely have a frozen pipe. The first step is to locate the frozen section. Look for visible frost on pipes, or feel for cold spots. You can try to thaw the pipe using a hairdryer, a heat lamp, or even warm towels, but never use an open flame. Apply heat slowly and gently. If the pipe has already burst, you'll see water actively leaking once it thaws. In this scenario, immediately turn off your main water shut-off valve to prevent further damage and call BRV Plumbing right away. Addressing frozen pipes promptly can help limit the extent of the damage.

    Long-Term Prevention Strategies

    Beyond immediate winterization, consider long-term solutions for your Alabama home. Upgrading insulation in crawl spaces and attics, installing frost-proof outdoor spigots, or even relocating particularly vulnerable pipe sections can offer lasting protection. For homes with older, galvanized steel pipes, which are more prone to freezing due to internal corrosion, considering a re-pipe to PEX or copper might be a wise investment. These materials have better freeze resistance and can significantly improve your home's plumbing longevity. A professional assessment from BRV Plumbing can help you plan for these larger projects, ensuring your home is prepared for many Alabama winters to come.
