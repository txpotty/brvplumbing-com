    Why a Sewer Camera Inspection Belongs on Every Alabama Home Inspection

    By Andrew Blake · August 13, 2026 · 5 min read

    Protecting Your Investment: The Value of a Sewer Camera Inspection

    Buying a home in Alabama is a big decision, and a standard home inspection covers a lot of ground. However, one critical area often overlooked, or simply not accessible with a visual inspection, is the main sewer line. This underground pipe, which carries all wastewater from your home to the municipal sewer system or septic tank, is literally out of sight and out of mind until there's a problem. That's why at BRV Plumbing, we strongly recommend a sewer camera inspection as an essential part of every home inspection in our great state.

    Alabama's climate and soil conditions, from the red clay of the northern regions to the sandy soils near the Gulf Coast, can be tough on buried pipes. Tree roots are a common culprit, seeking moisture and infiltrating even tiny cracks in older lines. Ground movement over time, or even just the natural settling of a home, can cause pipes to shift, crack, or collapse. Without a camera, these issues remain hidden, only to surface as costly emergencies after you've closed on your new home.

    What a Sewer Camera Inspection Reveals

    A sewer camera inspection uses a high-resolution, waterproof camera attached to a flexible rod. This camera is snaked through your home's main sewer cleanout, providing a live video feed of the pipe's interior. Our trained technicians can see exactly what's going on down there, identifying potential problems before they become major headaches. Here's what we're looking for:

    • Tree Root Intrusion: Roots are drawn to the moisture and nutrients inside sewer pipes. They can enter through small cracks or loose joints, growing into a dense mass that blocks flow and eventually destroys the pipe. This is especially common in established neighborhoods with mature trees.
    • Cracks and Breaks: Pipes can crack from ground shifts, heavy loads above ground, or just plain old age. These cracks not only leak wastewater into the surrounding soil but also allow debris and roots to enter.
    • Corrosion or Deterioration: Older homes, especially those built before the 1970s, might have cast iron or clay pipes that are prone to corrosion, scaling, or crumbling over time. Our humid climate can accelerate some forms of pipe degradation.
    • Sagging or Bellies: A "belly" in a sewer line is a section where the pipe has sagged, creating a low spot where water and solid waste can collect. This leads to frequent clogs and eventual pipe failure.
    • Misaligned Joints: Over time, pipe sections can become disconnected or misaligned, creating ledges where waste can snag and accumulate, leading to blockages.
    • Foreign Objects: While less common in a pre-purchase inspection, sometimes foreign objects that shouldn't be in the sewer line can cause blockages or damage.

    The Cost of Neglect vs. Proactive Inspection

    Skipping a sewer camera inspection might seem like a way to save a few hundred dollars upfront. However, the potential costs of ignoring a hidden sewer line issue can be astronomical. A complete sewer line replacement, depending on the length of the line, the depth, and the type of landscaping or structures above it, can easily range from $5,000 to $25,000 or more. Imagine discovering that kind of expense just weeks after moving into your dream home in Alabama. A proactive inspection, typically costing between $200 and $500, is a small price to pay for such significant peace of mind.

    Furthermore, if an issue is found during the inspection, you have leverage as a buyer. You can negotiate with the seller to have the repair made before closing, ask for a credit, or even decide if the repair is too extensive for your comfort. This empowers you to make an informed decision rather than facing an unpleasant surprise later.

    When to Call a Professional

    We believe every home inspection in Alabama should include a sewer camera inspection, especially for homes that are more than 20 years old or those with mature trees on the property. If you're buying a home, or if you're a current homeowner experiencing slow drains, recurring clogs, or foul odors, don't wait for a complete backup.
